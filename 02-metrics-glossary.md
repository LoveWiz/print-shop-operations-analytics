# What the numbers mean — in plain language

Think of each shop day as a short story with two main characters:

**Expected** — what production and services say the day *should* look like.  
**Reported** — what the till and staff say actually came in.

The owner’s job is to see whether those two characters agree — and if not, why.

---

## The questions the dashboard answers

**“What should we have made?”**  
Printers and priced services set an expected picture. Binding, lamination, and other counter work get added when staff log them. Then we take away the things that aren’t clean revenue: discounts, error costs, day-to-day expenses, and intentional printouts that shouldn’t count as sales.

**“What did we take in?”**  
Staff report the day’s cash — card and physical — through the website. That becomes the reported side of the story.

**“Are we close?”**  
A **variance** percentage shows how far reported sits from expected. Small gaps are normal noise. Larger ones light up so the owner can ask questions while the day is still fresh — not weeks later in a paper pile.

**“Which shop? Which work?”**  
The same story can be told across **all three shops**, or one at a time, and broken into **service groups** so “printing had a good day” becomes specific: colored A4, comb binding, lamination, and so on.

**“Will we run out of paper or toner mid-rush?”**  
Stock against reorder points, plus alerts, keep inventory from becoming a surprise ending.

---

## Two ideas people often mix up

**All printer outputs** (copies, prints, scans) describe *what the machines did*.  
**Intentional printouts** are the ones marked on purpose — so the owner can leave them out of “final sales” instead of treating every click as money.

That distinction is how trust gets rebuilt: the report stops arguing with reality.

---

## What “performance” looks like here

For staff, “how did we do?” is mainly **this shop’s expected vs reported over recent days** — a shared scoreboard for the location, not a blame spreadsheet in the portfolio story. The owner still gets the multi-shop view and the deeper reconciliation when accounting needs it.

---

## Guides vs hard truths

- **Expected vs reported** is the hard comparison.  
- Some non-printer work also shows a **trend guide** (a rolling sense of “usual”) next to what was actually logged — helpful context, not a second invoice.

---

*If you only remember one sentence, this should be said sentence: The business stopped asking “who remembers yesterday?” and started asking “do expected and reported agree — and if not, what’s the reason?”*
