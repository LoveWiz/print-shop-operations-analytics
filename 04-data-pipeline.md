# From the shop floor to the owner’s screen

Every good ops story has a path. Here’s ours, without the plumbing diagram.

---

## Morning and night on the floor

Printers keep their own memory of copies, prints, and scans. Each day, that memory leaves the shop as a **file drop** from the PCs — and staff can upload when they need to.

Separately, people still know what the till feels like: cash in, discounts given, mistakes paid for, money spent, binding and other desk work sold, printouts done on purpose. They type that into **short web forms** instead of a paper sheet that might never leave the counter.

Both paths meet in **one database**. No more “the truth is in the binder at shop two.”

---

## Turning activity into a fair day’s picture

Machine counts become **expected sales** once they’re priced against the real service list.  
Staff entries become **reported cash and costs**.

Then the story gets honest about exceptions: discounts, errors, expenses, and intentional work come **off** the headline sales number so the owner isn’t congratulating the shop for free prints.

If a day (or two) of counter readings never arrives, we don’t pretend. A **baseline reset** says: start counting cleanly from here — keep the history, don’t invent the gap.

Odd job logs can wait in a **review** step so a bad parse doesn’t silently become “expected revenue.”

---

## What leaves the system

The owner gets a **living dashboard** and, when needed, a printable or downloadable pack for accounting.

Shop staff get a calmer close: enter, upload, glance at how their shop is tracking.

That’s the whole pipeline: **capture → one source of truth → a story people can act on the same day.**
